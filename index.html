Which weekend?

28-29 November wins: it is the only November weekend with every big market open, including Heinzels and its ice rink, which opens on 23 November. Open the doors to see why.

Skip it

No Christmas markets open yet.

Too early

Only the Harbour market and Stadtgarten (from 13 Nov). The Dom market opens 16 Nov.

Second choice

Saturday is good and a little quieter. No Heinzels ice rink yet, and on Sunday 22 Nov (Totensonntag) most markets stay shut until 18:00.

Our pick
Go now

Everything open: Dom, Heinzels with skating, Neumarkt, Rudolfplatz, Harbour, Stadtgarten, Chlodwigplatz, MediaPark. Busiest weekend of the month, as Sunday 29 is the first Advent.

Tips for 28-29 Nov: book now, do the Dom market on Saturday before 14:00 or late in the evening, and bring cash because not every stall takes cards.

Sample plan

Leave early so the Dom market happens before the afternoon crush. The car stays parked all weekend: the centre is walkable and the Stadtbahn does the rest.

Saturday 28 November

  1. 07:30Leave Rotterdam, about 260 km and 2.5 to 3 hours. Park at the stay, drop bags.
  2. 11:00-13:00Dom market (open from 10:00), maybe the cathedral inside.
  3. 13:00-16:00Brauhaus lunch (Früh or Päffgen). Kiara shops Ehrenstraße; Liz can lunch at Le Moissonnier Bistro.
  4. 16:00-18:30Heinzels: skating or ice curling, Glühwein, bratwurst.
  5. 19:00-22:00Dinner: fine dining or a brauhaus. Or the Dom view from the Rheinboulevard in Deutz.
  6. 22:00-01:00Cocktails at Little Link or Seiberts.
  7. 01:00 onwardsClub: Bootshaus, Elektroküche or fi (check line-ups). Taxi home.

Sunday 29 November

  1. MorningSleep in, brunch in the Belgian Quarter or Ehrenfeld.
  2. 11:00-13:00Museum Ludwig or Kolumba.
  3. 13:00-16:00Rheinauhafen and the Kranhäuser, then the Harbour market by the Chocolate Museum.
  4. 16:00Last Glühwein, then drive home before the Sunday evening border traffic.

Find two or three more yourselves

On Airbnb: Cologne, 28-29 November, 5 guests, then filter on free parking on premises, at least 3 bedrooms and Superhost or Guest favourite. Look at the Belgian Quarter, Neustadt-Nord, Deutz and Ehrenfeld on the map.

  • Cologne listings must show a registration number near the bottom. No number is a red flag.
  • Cologne charges a 5% bed tax, sometimes paid separately.
  • "Street parking only" means circling: on an Advent Saturday in the centre that's close to hopeless.

Driving and parking

About 260 km and 2.5 to 3 hours from Rotterdam (A15/A73 or A12/A3), plus a fuel stop. Check that five people and their bags fit in one car.

Green sticker required. Cologne's low-emission zone (Umweltzone) covers the whole centre, including the Belgian Quarter and Deutz, 24/7. Without a green Umweltplakette the fine is €100, Dutch plates included. Order it online or from TÜV/Dekra (about €5-20) and allow a few days for delivery. Germany's new exemption for electric cars only covers German E-plates, so you need the sticker for an EV too.

Parking, three ways

  • At the stay: a private spot or the car park the host suggests. The car doesn't move all weekend.
  • City-centre garages: Dom (420 spaces), Hauptbahnhof (395), Philharmonie (375), Groß St. Martin (163) per koeln.de. Often full from 11:00 and pricey for 24 hours.
  • Edge of town or Park+Ride: the city recommends Kaiser-Wilhelm-Ring, MediaPark or the Lanxess Arena, or P+R with the Stadtbahn in (Stadt Köln). MediaPark is walkable from the Belgian Quarter.

Agree on the designated driver early: Germany also uses a 0.5 per mille limit, and 0.0 for new drivers.
